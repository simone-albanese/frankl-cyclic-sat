# Z15 — partial result record (run stopped without verdict)

**Status: NOT decided to this project's standard.** The project requires either
two independent exact methods in agreement, or one method plus a verified proof
certificate. For Z15 (rotation-invariant union-closed families on 15 points,
member sets of size ≥ 3) we have exactly **one** completed exact method.

## What is established

1. **CP-SAT (OR-Tools 9.15.6755), monolithic model** — task T9a, 2026-08-11:
   `{"status": "INFEASIBLE", "m": 15}` in ~15 minutes (889 s solve time) under
   a 9 GB RAM cap. Model: 2,190 nontrivial cyclic orbits, 28,772,876 closure
   clauses, scaled-margin constraint `Σ r_O(2s_O−15)x_O ≤ −15`, orbits of sets
   smaller than 3 forced to 0.
   Log: `results/logs/T9a_z15_cpsat_min3.log`.

   Taken at face value, this says: **no rotation-invariant counterexample to
   Frankl's conjecture exists on 15 points.** But a single solver's word is
   exactly what this project's protocol refuses to accept as final.

## Why the run was stopped

A read-only feasibility analysis on the morning of 2026-08-12 concluded:

- Probability of a verdict before the watchdog deadline (15 h total budget),
  from a heavy-tailed survival model over the measured trajectory: **~10–15%**.
- Even on success, certificate verification was projected out of reach on this
  machine: drat-trim on the projected ~14 GiB proof was estimated to need
  **11–18 GB of RAM** (two independent estimation methods, calibrated on the
  measured Z14 verification), against 16 GB of physical memory — with swap,
  runtime becomes unbounded in practice.

The bottleneck had stopped being CPU time and had become verifier memory.
The owner chose to stop the run cleanly and publish, leaving Z15 as an open,
precisely-documented challenge. See `docs/open-problems.md` for the concrete
plan (sharding into 2^k independent subproblems) that any contributor with
more RAM — or more patience — can pick up.
